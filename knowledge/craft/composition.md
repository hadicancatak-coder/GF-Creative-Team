# Composition — making the frame do work

## The one rule under all of the others
**One element owns the frame.** Decide which before you place anything. Everything else is support, and
support means smaller, quieter, or further from the optical centre. A frame where three things are
roughly equal is a frame with no subject, and it reads as a template.

## Choose a layout system, and only one
- **Single column, left-aligned.** The default for type-led work. Honest, fast to read, hard to make
  ugly. Everything shares one left edge — that edge is the composition.
- **Two-part split.** Frame divided at a stated ratio — 1:2, 2:3, 1:1. One part carries the subject, one
  carries the words. The division must be **hard and deliberate**, not a drifting gap.
- **Full-bleed with an inset.** Image or field fills the frame; type sits in one reserved corner or band.
  The inset needs a contrast guarantee: a scrim, a solid, or a region of the image verified quiet.
- **Centred axis.** Everything on one vertical line. Use only when symmetry *means* something —
  ceremony, formality, a single object. It is the weakest default and the commonest one.
- **Diagonal or offset.** The subject off-axis, tension held by counterweight. Powerful, easy to get
  wrong; needs a real counterweight, not empty space pretending to be one.

Two systems in one frame is the most common structural failure. A centred logo above a left-aligned
stack is two systems.

## Proportion — what the numbers should be

- **The dominant element takes 40–70% of the frame — by AREA, and only when it has area to take.**
  Under 30% and nothing owns it; over 80% and there is nowhere for the eye to rest. This applies to
  objects, images and fields.

  **It does not apply to type-led work, and applying it there produces a false failure.** Set at a
  typical display ceiling, a two-line headline's *ink* cannot exceed ~10–12% of a feed canvas however
  large you set it — the glyphs simply do not cover that much surface. For a STATEMENT ad, dominance is
  **contrast and exclusive occupancy**: the element is the highest-contrast thing in the frame, and it
  holds a region of the frame alone. Measure it that way and say which measure you used. A reviewer who
  fails a type-led frame for 11% area dominance has measured the wrong thing.
- **The message must outweigh the decoration** — this one holds for every pattern, by area. Measure both as a share of frame. If ornament, texture or
  pattern exceeds the thing doing the persuading, the composition is inverted — and no token check will
  ever tell you, because every value can be legal while the proportions are wrong.
- **Total empty space: 25–40% of the frame.** Below 25% it is crowded. Above 40% it is under-filled and
  reads as unfinished — and distributed emptiness hides from per-region caps, so measure the *total*.
- **No single empty region above ~20% of canvas**, especially when open to the edge on two sides. That is
  a hole, not composed space. Composed space is bounded by content and is doing a job.

## Optical over mathematical
- Optical centre sits **~5% above** mathematical centre. Centring by arithmetic looks low.
- Type looks centred when its *visual* mass is centred — ignore the bounding box, ignore descenders on a
  single line.
- Round shapes need to be **~2% larger** than square ones to look the same size.
- Equal numeric margins around a text block look unequal; the optical top margin wants slightly more.
- When optics and arithmetic disagree, **trust the eye and record the deviation.**

## Weight and the eye path
Decide where the eye **enters**, where it **travels**, and where it **rests**. Then check the frame
actually does that.

- Entry is the highest-contrast, largest, or most saturated element — whichever is strongest. You do not
  get to choose entry by intention; the frame chooses it by physics.
- The CTA should be the **rest**, not the entry. If the only saturated object in a frame is the button,
  the eye arrives at the ask before the case — an inverted order of arrival.
- Weight distributes by **area × contrast**. A small saturated element can outweigh a large quiet one.

## Spacing
- Gaps come from a scale. Every gap. A single off-scale value is visible even when nobody can name why.
- **Related things sit closer than unrelated things.** This does more for legibility than any alignment
  rule — a caption 32px from its claim and 96px from the next block reads instantly as belonging.
- Margins are a **minimum clear zone**, not a target. Exceeding them is fine; crossing them is not.

## The thumbnail test — run it before you finish, not after
Render at ~110px. Name the one thing that survives.

- If nothing survives, the ad does not exist in a feed.
- If what survives is **decoration rather than the message**, the ad is backwards.
- If only the CTA survives, you have built a button with context attached.

Display type at 96px on a 1800px canvas is ~5.9px at thumbnail — **illegible**. If the proposition is
verbal, it must be set large enough to survive, or the frame needs a non-verbal carrier.
